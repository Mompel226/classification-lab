Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 · Topic 1, Characteristics and classification of living organisms

In one minute

  • Click any branch of the tree, or any step on the bar along the bottom. The tree lights the groups a station is about, and a group you click opens the station that teaches it.
  • Each station has two tabs: Learn — what you must know, with something to click, press or try in every section — and Practise, the questions.
  • Check as often as you like. You are told which parts are wrong, never the answer — you have to work it out. Keep going until it is all right, then Hand in.
  • An underlined word can be clicked: 🔍 opens a picture there and then, ↗ jumps to the station that explains it and brings you back.
  • Your work is saved in this browser on this device. Use the same computer each time, and hand in before you clear your browsing data.

The answers are never shown. To hand your work to Dr Mompel you sign in with your school Google account, so he knows whose it is; anyone else is welcome to use the lab and will still get a completion code.

What the colours mean
  • The seven characteristics each wear a lettered chip — M movement, R respiration, S sensitivity, G growth, R reproduction, E excretion, N nutrition — the letters of MRS GREN, so the colour is never the only signal.
  • A group name is coloured by its kingdom, in the same colour the tree uses to light it: animals, plants, fungi, prokaryotes, protoctists. Viruses stay grey, because they are in no kingdom.
  • Words about naming — species, genus, binomial — are purple.
  • A green-bordered box is the mistake students most often make there. A small S badge means Supplement (Paper 4 only). “Beyond the syllabus” is worth knowing but not named in 0610.
  • The colour key sits at the top of every Learn tab, so you never have to remember.
What is simplified here
  • The tree shows only the groups the syllabus names. Real trees of life have millions of branches, and mosses, conifers, snails and worms are all missing here on purpose.
  • The DNA sequences are short stretches of one gene, simplified so the differences can be counted on a screen. Real comparisons use thousands of bases and a computer.
  • The five kingdoms are the classification 0610 examines. Modern biology uses three domains and a great many more groups; the kingdoms are still the right answer in the exam.
  • The drawings are of specimens drawn from the photographs on the tree, not of a specimen in front of you. The rules are the same.
